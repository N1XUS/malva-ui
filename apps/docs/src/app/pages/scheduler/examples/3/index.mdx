## Drag, resize, callbacks and vetoes

With `editable` (default `true`) chips move with the pointer (SortableJS under the hood) and resize
from a handle on **either** edge; the grips appear on hover and keyboard focus, and stay visible
where there is no hover. Keyboard users move a chip with <kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>Arrow</kbd>, resize its
**end** edge with <kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Arrow</kbd>, and its **start** edge
with <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>Arrow</kbd> (<kbd>⌃⌥Arrow</kbd> on macOS). On a touch
screen a swipe scrolls both axes and a drag, resize or range selection arms on a short press
instead. The model updates optimistically, then `eventMove` / `eventResize` emit the payload
`{ event, previous, source }`. Return `false` from `canMove` / `canResize` to veto a change before
it lands — both hooks receive the already-applied `next` range, so `canResize` sees a start-edge
drag exactly as it sees an end-edge one — and the chip snaps back with a polite announcement
explaining why. Per-event `draggable: false` / `resizable: false` opt single events out.
