## Drag, resize, callbacks and vetoes

With `editable` (default `true`) chips move with the pointer (SortableJS under the hood) and resize
from a handle on **either** edge; the grips appear on hover and keyboard focus,
and stay visible where there is no hover. Keyboard users press <kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>Arrow</kbd> to move,

<kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Arrow</kbd> to resize the **end** edge and
<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>Arrow</kbd> (<kbd>⌃⌥Arrow</kbd> on macOS) to
resize the **start** edge. On a touch screen a swipe scrolls both axes and a
drag, resize or range selection arms on a short press instead. The model updates
optimistically, then `eventMove` / `eventResize` emit `
{(event, previous, source)}`. Return `false` from `canMove` / `canResize` to
veto a change before it lands — both hooks receive the already-applied `next`
range, so `canResize` sees a start-edge drag exactly as it sees an end-edge one
— and the chip snaps back with a polite announcement explaining why. Per-event
`draggable: false` / `resizable: false` opt single events out.
