---
title: Transitions, locks, and a drop policy
---

Four gates decide whether one slot accepts a card, and a drag session evaluates them **in this order**, recording the first that fails:

1. `locked` — the card is in `lockedItemIds`, or its source or target column or lane carries `locked: true`.
2. `transition` — `transitions` is set and holds no `{ from, to }` pair for that column change. Include the same-column pair (`{ from: 'draft', to: 'draft' }`) if reordering inside a column should stay possible.
3. `wip` — a column, group, or lane limit would be exceeded. **Active** carries `wipLimit: 2` and already holds two cards, so its count pill renders in the warning tone (`data-mlv-taskboard-wip="at-limit"`) and a third card is refused here before `canDropFn` is ever asked.
4. `policy` — your `canDropFn` returned `false`.

That recorded reason is the exported `MlvTaskboardDenialReason` union, and it is what the keyboard announcement reads out, so what the board says can never disagree with what it refused.

`canDropFn` is consulted for **every** candidate slot a drag enumerates, not once per drop — keep it synchronous, cheap, and free of side effects. It receives the dragged card's context and the target: `target.items` is the target bucket's visible cards **with the dragged card removed**, and `target.index` counts that list, so `index === target.items.length` means "after the last card". Use `beforeMove` (example 8) when the answer needs a round trip or a confirmation.

A move that never lands leaves `items` referentially unchanged and emits `moveCancelled` with an `MlvTaskboardMoveCancelReason` — `invalid-drop` for a slot that was never permitted, `cancelled` for an abandoned drag, the two `before-move-*` reasons for a guarded move, and `stale` when the application replaced `items` or `columns` between the start of the move and its commit.

Inputs used: `transitions`, `lockedItemIds`, `canDropFn`. Outputs used: `moveCancelled`.
