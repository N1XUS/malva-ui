---
title: Across browser tabs
---

The same editor over a `BroadcastChannel` transport. Open this page in a
second tab of the same browser: each tab joins one channel named after
`collaborationDocumentId`, and edits, carets and presence flow between them.
The transport is about twenty lines — open a channel, subscribe to the
outbound frames, emit `connected`, forward inbound messages, close on
unsubscribe — and it is the whole contract a WebSocket, WebRTC or pub/sub
transport meets too. Subscribe before emitting `connected`: the handshake
goes out the moment `connected` arrives, and a transport that reports it
first loses it and never syncs.

A tab waits for its first sync before it lets the user edit, so two clients
never fork the seed. A relay has no server to answer, so a tab that is alone
on the channel has nobody to sync with: after `collaborationSyncTimeout`
(1.5 s here, 10 s by default) it reports offline and stays empty and
read-only, since the seed lands only at a first sync, and it syncs and turns
editable as soon as a second tab arrives. With a stateful server that
answers every client, the wait ends at the first round trip.

Every peer's name and colour are validated on receipt: a name is trimmed and
capped, a colour must be `#rrggbb` or falls back to the palette
(`MLV_EDITOR_COLLABORATION_COLORS`), and both reach the page only as text or a
custom property. Content arriving from a peer passes the same image URL policy
as local input. None of this authenticates anyone: who may join a document is
the transport's decision.
